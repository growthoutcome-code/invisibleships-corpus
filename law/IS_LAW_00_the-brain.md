# The Brain: where the law stands

The Brain is this archive's reference to the law around artificial intelligence, neural data, surveillance and the rules of war. Everything added to the site is read against it: news, analysis, concepts and the glossary. For each law it says what the law covers, whether it reaches government use, and what it does not cover.

This is not legal advice. Whether a law applies to a situation is a question for a lawyer.

## How it is written

- **Facts come from official texts:** the law, the order or the court record, linked at the end of each entry.
- **Analysis is labelled as analysis** and credited to the scholar who wrote it. Their published work is summarised on the News page under Analysis.
- **Every entry says what the law does not cover.** Most AI and neural-data laws regulate how companies handle data. Few reach government use. None we have found addresses technology delivering communication to a person without consent.
- **It is updated** when the news shows a law passing, changing, being blocked or being enforced. Each file shows the date it was last updated.

## The site's stance

This site reports the news and what others say, credited to them. It holds no official opinion, with two exceptions: what a person can do to improve safety at home and at work, and that it stands against Zersetzung tactics, which are criminal, including in any government cloud.

## Three ways of governing AI

The law professor Nita Farahany describes the European Union, the United States and China as pursuing different goals rather than one race (*Thinking Freely*, 1 December 2025):

- **The European Union sets the floor.** It regulates rights first and sorts AI by use: some uses are banned, some are high-risk and heavily regulated, others carry transparency duties. Its rules spread because companies that sell in Europe follow them everywhere.
- **The United States holds the frontier.** It relies on technological leadership, export controls, sector regulators and executive orders rather than one federal AI law, and the federal government is pushing back against state AI laws.
- **China makes itself unavoidable.** It governs AI through foundational data laws, targeted rules for each technology and technical standards, with model registration and real-name traceability, while offering free AI models abroad and controlling key materials.

## When a privacy law does not apply, criminal law still does

Several neural-data laws exempt government handling of data. That exemption is about data-handling rules. It does not make crimes lawful. In the United States, harassment and stalking are crimes for anyone, including people working for a government; it is a federal crime for two or more people to conspire to injure or intimidate someone in exercising their rights (18 U.S.C. § 241), and for anyone acting under official authority to deprive a person of their rights (18 U.S.C. § 242); and people can sue officials who violate their rights under state authority (42 U.S.C. § 1983).

# Crime

No law names Zersetzung, and none names harm done with neurotechnology. The methods are still crimes: following, threatening, intimidating, breaking in, tampering with evidence. When privacy law does not apply, criminal law still does. This section summarises what the site's research has documented and links to it. It does not repeat it.

## Its methods have been punished in court

In August 2019 members of eBay's security team ran a campaign against the publishers of a newsletter in Natick, Massachusetts, that was critical of the company: threatening deliveries, the couple's address posted with invitations to strangers, threats sent under invented identities, surveillance and a planned break-in to put a tracker on their car, followed by deleted evidence and lies to police. Seven former employees were convicted; the security director was sentenced to 57 months in prison. The company admitted the facts in a deferred prosecution agreement and paid a $3 million criminal penalty. The couple's civil suit settled in July 2026 for a reported $55.7 million.

- **Sources:** US Attorney, District of Massachusetts: [the sentencing of two former executives](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ma/pr/two-former-ebay-executives-sentenced-prison-cyberstalking), [the company's $3 million penalty](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ma/pr/ebay-inc-pay-3-million-connection-corporate-cyberstalking-campaign-targeting) and [the final sentencing](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ma/pr/final-defendant-ebay-cyberstalking-case-sentenced).

## Organised covert harassment is established fact

Sustained, deniable targeting of named people is not a theory and not confined to states. The site's research records seven decided or settled cases, by police forces and by corporations, each with its court or regulator.

## Terrorism, counterterrorism and military strategy

- **Terrorism:** under U.S. law, violent acts dangerous to human life that break criminal law and appear intended to intimidate or coerce a civilian population, to influence a government's policy by intimidation or coercion, or to affect a government's conduct by mass destruction, assassination or kidnapping ([18 U.S.C. § 2331](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2331)). The definition describes the act and its aim. It does not depend on who commits it. See [terrorism](https://www.invisibleships.com/glossary/terrorism) in the glossary.
- **Counterterrorism:** action against terrorism. It is bound by the law it enforces. The UN Security Council requires every counterterrorism measure to comply with international human rights, refugee and humanitarian law ([Resolution 2178, 2014](https://undocs.org/S/RES/2178(2014))). In armed conflict, civilians may never be the object of attack, and violence meant to spread terror among them is prohibited ([Additional Protocol I, Article 51(2)](https://www.icrc.org/en/document/what-does-ihl-say-about-terrorism)). An operation that deliberately attacks civilians breaks these rules, whoever carries it out. See [counterterrorism](https://www.invisibleships.com/glossary/counterterrorism).
- **Military strategy:** a plan for using armed force, alone or with a state's other powers, to reach an objective (DOD Dictionary of Military and Associated Terms). As this site uses the term, a military strategy can include a series of events arranged to prompt a further event. See [military strategy](https://www.invisibleships.com/glossary/military-strategy).

## The laws that apply

**United States, federal:**

- **Stalking** by interstate travel or by electronic means: [18 U.S.C. § 2261A](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2261A).
- **Conspiracy** to commit an offence: 18 U.S.C. § 371.
- **Conspiracy against rights:** two or more people who conspire to injure or intimidate someone in exercising their rights, [18 U.S.C. § 241](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/241).
- **Deprivation of rights under official authority:** 18 U.S.C. § 242; and a person can sue officials who violate their rights under state authority, 42 U.S.C. § 1983.
- **Computer intrusion:** the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, [18 U.S.C. § 1030](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1030).
- **Covering it up:** witness tampering, 18 U.S.C. § 1512; destroying or falsifying records, 18 U.S.C. § 1519.

**United States, states (two examples):**

- **Massachusetts:** criminal harassment, [M.G.L. c. 265 § 43A](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartIV/TitleI/Chapter265/Section43A); stalking, [c. 265 § 43](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartIV/TitleI/Chapter265/Section43).
- **Colorado:** stalking, [C.R.S. 18-3-602](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_18-3-602); harassment, [C.R.S. 18-9-111](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_18-9-111).

**Germany:** Zersetzung was not prosecuted as such after 1990, because laws cannot punish acts retroactively. Its targets can instead be formally rehabilitated, and since 2019 anyone recognised as the target of a Zersetzung measure receives a one-off payment of €1,500 ([Bundesstiftung zur Aufarbeitung der SED-Diktatur](https://www.bundesstiftung-aufarbeitung.de/de/erinnern/opfer-und-betroffene/juristische-aufarbeitung/rehabilitierungsgesetze)).

**What these laws do not answer:** how a target shows a coordinated campaign when no single act looks criminal on its own, and who investigates when the conduct crosses state or national lines.

Official statistics count arrests, homicide, break-ins and who is held in detention. ## Where to report

- **A crime in progress or a threat to safety:** local police, or 911 in the United States.
- **Stalking, threats or harassment across state lines or online:** the FBI, at [tips.fbi.gov](https://tips.fbi.gov); crimes committed online can also be reported to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center, at [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov).
- **A rights violation by someone acting under official authority:** the Justice Department's Civil Rights Division, at [civilrights.justice.gov](https://civilrights.justice.gov).
- **Keep a dated record** of each incident, with whatever evidence exists, before reporting. A record made at the time is what an investigator can work from.

# The Brain: China

## Three layers of AI governance

- **Foundational laws:** the Cybersecurity Law (2017, amended 2025), the Data Security Law (2021) and the Personal Information Protection Law (2021), which requires separate consent for sensitive personal information.
- **Targeted rules for each technology:** rules on algorithmic recommendation (2022), on "deep synthesis" such as deepfakes (2023), and interim measures on generative AI services (2023). Public AI models are registered with the Cyberspace Administration of China before release, users are verified by real name, and content must be traceable.
- **Technical standards:** the AI Security Governance Framework 2.0 (September 2025). It is not binding, but such standards are often folded into binding rules later.

## What it means for a person there

Companies need consent for sensitive personal data, and AI content is tightly controlled and traceable. Those consent rules govern companies; they do not limit state access in the way European or American rights do.

## Analysis

Nita Farahany (*Thinking Freely*, 1 December 2025) describes China's approach as targeted rules for specific technologies that let regulators move fast at the cost of predictability, coordinated across agencies led by the Cyberspace Administration. Abroad, China offers free AI models, proposes a World AI Cooperation Organization based in Shanghai, and controls rare-earth materials needed for chips. She has also noted that China pairs investment in brain-computer interfaces with research on cognitive warfare and robotics (September 2025).

# The law: Latin America

Latin America moved first on neurorights. Chile wrote them into its constitution, a Brazilian state followed, and several countries have bills pending. Most of these provisions protect brain activity and the data drawn from it as a right of the person, not only as consumer data, and some are written broadly enough to reach the state as well as companies.

## Chile

### Constitutional reform, Law 21.383
- **Status:** promulgated 14 October 2021; published in the *Diario Oficial* on 25 October 2021. In force.
- **What it says:** it adds to Article 19 No. 1 of the Constitution (the right to life and to physical and psychological integrity) that scientific and technological development shall serve people and respect life and physical and psychological integrity, and that the law shall regulate the requirements, conditions and restrictions on its use in people, "especially safeguarding brain activity and the information that comes from it".
- **Government use:** a constitutional right binds the state as well as private parties.
- **What it does not cover:** it leaves the detail to a law. A companion neuroprotection bill (Bulletin 13.828-19) was introduced with the reform; we have not found it enacted.
- **Enforced:** yes, once. In August 2023 the Supreme Court ordered the company Emotiv to delete a former senator's brain data and asked the health and customs authorities to check the device's sale and use (Rol 105.065-2023).
- **Analysis:** a post on Stanford Law School's Law and Biosciences Blog (["Even Chile's Neurorights Leave Inferred Mental Data in a Gray Zone"](https://law.stanford.edu/2026/04/27/even-chiles-neurorights-leave-inferred-mental-data-in-a-gray-zone/), 27 April 2026) argues that even Chile's protection leaves mental data *inferred* by AI from other sources in a grey zone.
- **Sources:** [Law 21.383, *Diario Oficial* No. 43.086-B](https://www.diariooficial.interior.gob.cl/publicaciones/2021/10/25/43086-B/01/2031873.pdf); Poder Judicial de Chile, [press release of 11 August 2023](https://www.pjud.cl/prensa-y-comunicaciones/noticias-del-poder-judicial/96951).

## Brazil

### Rio Grande do Sul: state constitution
- **Status:** neurorights added to the state constitution, December 2023 (reported date of enactment: 20 December 2023). In force.
- **What it says:** it makes mental integrity a principle of the state constitution.
- **To verify:** we have not yet read the amendment's official text; the details above come from the [NeuroRights Foundation](https://www.neurorightsfoundation.org/advocacy/latin-america) and the [Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/privacy-and-the-rise-of-neurorights-in-latin-america/).

### Federal proposals (pending)
- **Proposed Constitutional Amendment 29/2023 (PEC 29/2023):** would add to Article 5 of the Federal Constitution the protection of mental integrity and algorithmic transparency. Introduced in the Senate in June 2023; still pending.
- **Bill 2,174/2023:** a catalogue of rights and prohibitions concerning the brain and nervous system. Pending.
- **Bill 522/2022:** would add neural data to Brazil's general data-protection law (LGPD). Pending.
- **Sources:** Senado Federal, [PEC 29/2023](https://www25.senado.leg.br/web/atividade/materias/-/materia/158095); Moreira and Vilaça, [*Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* (2026)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2026.1873948/full).

## Mexico

- **Charter of Digital Rights (2023):** a charter issued by Mexico's federal data-protection authority recognises the five neurorights proposed by the NeuroRights Foundation. It is guidance, not law.
- **Proposals (pending):** a constitutional reform that would protect personal identity and physical and psychological integrity, in wording close to Chile's; a reform that would let Congress legislate on AI, cybersecurity and neurorights; and a draft General Law on Neurorights and Neurotechnologies in the Senate (2024).
- **Sources:** Future of Privacy Forum, ["Privacy and the Rise of 'Neurorights' in Latin America"](https://fpf.org/blog/privacy-and-the-rise-of-neurorights-in-latin-america/) (20 March 2024); [Moreira and Vilaça (2026)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2026.1873948/full).

## Argentina

- **Misiones province:** a provincial neurorights law protecting neural activity, neural data and mental autonomy is reported as adopted in 2023. **To verify:** the source cited for it is labelled a bill.
- **Federal proposals (pending):** Bill 339-D-2022 (neurotechnology as evidence in court, with safeguards) and Bill 2446/23 (a congressional committee to draft a neurorights framework).

## Other countries with proposals

- **Colombia:** an update to the data-protection law with a section on neural data.
- **Costa Rica:** a proposal to treat neural data as biometric data.
- **Uruguay:** Parliament has discussed regulating neurotechnology and protecting neural data.
- **Source:** [Future of Privacy Forum (20 March 2024)](https://fpf.org/blog/privacy-and-the-rise-of-neurorights-in-latin-america/). Status of each to be rechecked.

## Regional instruments (not binding)

- **Organization of American States:** the Inter-American Juridical Committee's Declaration on neuroscience, neurotechnologies and human rights (2021), and its Inter-American Declaration of Principles (2023).
- **Latin American and Caribbean Parliament (PARLATINO):** a model law on neurorights (2023) that member countries can adapt, with an independent oversight authority and remedies.

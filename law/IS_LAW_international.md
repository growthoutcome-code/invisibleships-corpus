# The law: international

No treaty yet addresses neurotechnology. International bodies have adopted principles and asked for guidelines; none of them binds a country by itself.

## United Nations Human Rights Council

### Resolution 51/3, "Neurotechnology and human rights"
- **Status:** adopted 6 October 2022.
- **What it does:** asked the Council's Advisory Committee to study the impact of neurotechnology on human rights. The Committee's report was presented at the Council's 57th session (2024).

### Resolution 58/6, "Neurotechnology and human rights"
- **Status:** adopted without a vote on 2 April 2025.
- **What it does:** asks the Advisory Committee to draft recommended guidelines on applying existing human rights law to neurotechnology, from design to use, for the Council's 64th session; invites the High Commissioner, the treaty bodies and the special rapporteurs to consider neurotechnology in their work.
- **Binding:** no. It requests, welcomes and invites; the guidelines are still to come.
- **Sources:** [A/HRC/RES/51/3](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/3991860); [A/HRC/RES/58/6](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4082164/files/A_HRC_RES_58_6-EN.pdf).

## UNESCO

### Recommendation on the Ethics of Neurotechnology
- **Status:** adopted by UNESCO's General Conference in November 2025.
- **What it covers:** the first global standard on neurotechnology ethics: human dignity, freedom of thought, mental privacy and the protection of neural data, with particular care for children and for uses at work.
- **Binding:** no. A UNESCO recommendation asks member states to act; it is not a treaty.
- **Sources:** [UNESCO, final draft text](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000393266); [UK National Commission for UNESCO, "UNESCO adopts first global framework on neurotechnology ethics"](https://unesco.org.uk/news/unesco-adopts-first-global-framework-on-neurotechnology-ethics) (18 November 2025).
- **Analysis:** writing on Stanford Law School's Law and Biosciences Blog ([30 March 2026](https://law.stanford.edu/2026/03/30/who-owns-digital-thoughts-the-limits-of-property-law-and-the-2025-unesco-recommendation-on-the-ethics-of-neurotechnology/)), Bo Hyoung Lee describes it as a human-rights framework that still relies on consent.

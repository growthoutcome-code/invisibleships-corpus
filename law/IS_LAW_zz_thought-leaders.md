# The thought leaders this site follows

These are the public writers on neuroethics, neurorights, AI law and privacy whose published work this site reads every day and summarises on the News page, credited to them. They are named here as authors of their own published work, as news media name them. Following them is not an endorsement, and they have no connection to this site.

- **Nita Farahany:** professor of law and philosophy at Duke University; author of *The Battle for Your Brain*; writes *Thinking Freely* ([nitafarahany.substack.com](https://nitafarahany.substack.com)), including her AI Law & Policy course. [Her summaries on News](https://www.invisibleships.com/news?by=Nita%20Farahany)
- **Daniel J. Solove:** professor of privacy law at George Washington University Law School; writes *Solove on Tech* ([danielsolove.substack.com](https://danielsolove.substack.com)). [His summaries on News](https://www.invisibleships.com/news?by=Daniel%20J.%20Solove)
- **Future of Privacy Forum:** a privacy research group whose neural-data work includes Jameson Spivack's comparisons of state neural-data laws ([fpf.org](https://fpf.org)). [Summaries on News](https://www.invisibleships.com/news?by=Jameson%20Spivack)
- **Stanford Law School, Law and Biosciences Blog:** writing from the Center for Law and the Biosciences, including Bo Hyoung Lee on the ownership of neural data ([law.stanford.edu](https://law.stanford.edu/law-and-biosciences-blog/)). [Summary on News](https://www.invisibleships.com/news?by=Bo%20Hyoung%20Lee)
- **Tech Policy Press:** including Courtney C. Radsch on cognitive liberty and corporate AI ([techpolicy.press](https://techpolicy.press)). [Summary on News](https://www.invisibleships.com/news?by=Courtney%20C.%20Radsch)
- **The NeuroRights Foundation:** founded by the neuroscientist Rafael Yuste; proposed the five neurorights and tracks neurorights law ([neurorightsfoundation.org](https://neurorightsfoundation.org)).

Their summaries on this site are marked *Analysis* on the News page. Pick a name under **Thought leader** in the News filter or chart to see only theirs.

## How we read the news

The News is gathered every day, around the clock, by an AI-assisted watch of official releases and reporting worldwide. Here is exactly what that watch does, and what it does not.

**What it does:**

- **Chooses the sources:** official releases (courts, prosecutors, regulators, legislatures) first, then reporting and trade press, then the thought leaders above.
- **Summarises each item** in plain words, credited to its source and linked to the original. Charges are reported as allegations unless a person pleaded guilty, was convicted or was sentenced.
- **Tags each item by topic** (neurorights, neuroethics, mental privacy, AI law, rules of war) with fixed rules based on the item's own words and categories, so the same item is always tagged the same way.
- **Checks laws against this page.** When an item shows a law passing, changing, being blocked or being enforced, the entry for that law is updated.

**What it does not do:** it does not write opinions or conclusions of its own about the news. Commentary on this site is always someone else's, credited to them.

**The site's stance:** this site reports the news and what others say, credited to them. It holds no official opinion, with two exceptions: what a person can do to improve safety at home and at work, and that it stands against Zersetzung tactics, which are criminal, including in any government cloud.

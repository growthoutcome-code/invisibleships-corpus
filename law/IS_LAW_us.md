# The law: United States

**What protects neural data in the United States: state consumer-privacy laws, and nothing else yet.** Six states have made neural data "sensitive data" under their privacy laws: Colorado first (2024), then California, Montana, Connecticut, Delaware and Vermont. They work the same way: a business must ask a person's permission before collecting or using neural data, must assess the risks, and must let the person see and delete it. Each state's attorney general enforces its law and a person cannot sue under any of them. They exempt government, except Montana's, which requires a warrant or subpoena before government may obtain neural data.

**There is no federal law on neural data.** A bill, the MIND Act, would only ask the Federal Trade Commission to study it. Federal policy on AI is set mostly by executive orders. An executive order directs federal agencies; it does not create rights a person can enforce, and it cannot by itself cancel a state law, though it can send federal lawyers to challenge one in court, as has happened in Colorado.

## Neural data: Colorado

### Colorado Privacy Act: neural data (HB24-1058)
- **Status:** signed 17 April 2024; in effect since 6 August 2024.
- **What it covers:** adds biological and neural data (information from measuring the activity of a person's central or peripheral nervous system) to "sensitive data". Covered businesses need a person's **opt-in consent** before processing it and must assess the risks. A person can ask to see, correct and delete it.
- **When neural data counts:** the law defines biological data as data used, or meant to be used, to identify a person. Commentators read the neural-data part differently: some say neural data is covered only when used to identify someone (Future of Privacy Forum; *Inside BCI*, 3 April 2026), others that the neural-data definition carries no such condition (Hunton). The enacted text is the test.
- **Who has to comply:** businesses handling the data of 100,000 or more Colorado residents a year, or 25,000 or more while making money from selling data.
- **Government use:** **exempt** for data kept by the State, the courts, counties, cities and towns, and state colleges and universities, when collected and used as authorised by law for noncommercial purposes. A state law generally does not reach federal agencies.
- **Enforcement:** the Attorney General and district attorneys; up to $20,000 per violation. Individuals cannot sue under it.
- **Medical devices:** health information held by a hospital, doctor or insurer under HIPAA, the federal medical-privacy law, is outside the act (C.R.S. 6-1-1304(2)(a)). Many medical brain-computer interfaces, such as implants used in clinical care, would fall there rather than under this law.
- **Where to complain:** the Attorney General, at [coag.gov/cpa](https://coag.gov/cpa).
- **What it does not cover:** government use as above; medical data covered by HIPAA; what AI concludes from neural data; phones and other devices that infer a person's mental state from typing, voice or sleep.
- **Analysis:** Nita Farahany ([*Thinking Freely*, 5 August 2025](https://nitafarahany.substack.com/p/your-meditation-app-may-soon-have)) writes that the neural-data laws of California, Colorado, Montana and Connecticut protect the raw signal from the nervous system, not what AI infers from it.
- **Sources:** C.R.S. 6-1-1303 and [6-1-1304](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_6-1-1304) ([HB24-1058](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb24-1058)); Colorado Attorney General, [Colorado Privacy Act](https://coag.gov/cpa); Hunton, ["Colorado Amends Privacy Act with H.B. 1058"](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/colorado-amends-privacy-act-with-h-b-1058-adding-new-protections-for-biological-and-neural-data); *Inside BCI*, ["US States Build Patchwork of Neural Data Privacy Laws"](https://insidebci.com/policy/2026-04-03-us-states-build-patchwork-of-neural-data-privacy-laws-as-bci-market-accelerates/) (3 April 2026).

## Neural data: the other states

All of these are consumer-privacy laws: they govern businesses that handle residents' data, need consent (in most, opt-in) before neural data is processed, and are enforced by the state attorney general. None lets an individual sue. Only Montana's also limits what government may do (below).

| State | Law | Status | What counts as neural data |
|---|---|---|---|
| California | SB 1223 (amends the California Consumer Privacy Act) | signed 2024; in force | activity of the central or peripheral nervous system; excludes data inferred from non-neural sources |
| Montana | SB 163 (amends the Genetic Information Privacy Act) | signed 2025; in force since 1 October 2025 | central and peripheral nervous systems; government needs a search warrant or investigative subpoena; the data may not be stored in countries the U.S. sanctions |
| Connecticut | SB 1295 (amends the Connecticut Data Privacy Act) | signed 2025 | central nervous system only |
| Delaware | HB 380 (amends the Delaware Personal Data Privacy Act) | signed 2 September 2026; takes effect 1 January 2027 | central nervous system |
| Vermont | S.71, Vermont Data Privacy and Online Surveillance Act | enacted 2026; takes effect 1 January 2028 | central nervous system; separate consent needed to sell it |

- **What they do not cover:** what AI concludes about a person's mind from other data, such as typing, voice or sleep; government use.
- **Analysis:** Daniel J. Solove ([*Solove on Tech*, 8 October 2026](https://danielsolove.substack.com/p/rethinking-privacy-of-the-mind-why)) argues these laws fall short because many cover only the central nervous system, because consent and risk assessments are weak protections in practice, and because mental states can be inferred from non-neural data. Jameson Spivack and a co-author at the Future of Privacy Forum ([12 August 2025](https://fpf.org/blog/the-neural-data-goldilocks-problem-defining-neural-data-in-u-s-state-privacy-laws/)) compare the definitions.
- **Sources:** Risk Management Magazine, ["State of Mind: The New Landscape of Neural Data Privacy Laws"](https://www.rmmagazine.com/articles/article/2026/02/24/state-of-mind--the-new-landscape-of-neural-data-privacy-laws) (24 February 2026); Troutman Pepper Locke, ["Delaware Significantly Amends Its Consumer Data Privacy Law"](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/09/delaware-significantly-amends-its-consumer-data-privacy-law-what-businesses-need-to-know/) (September 2026); Cooley, ["From Maple to Mind Taps: New Vermont Law Puts Neurotech on Notice"](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-06-23-from-maple-to-mind-taps-new-vermont-law-puts-neurotech-on-notice) (23 June 2026). Official texts to be linked entry by entry.

## Neural data held by government

**Why government is exempt.** The state neural-data laws are consumer-privacy laws: they were written to regulate how businesses treat customers. Legislatures handle government's own records through other laws, so these acts leave them out. Colorado's, for example, does not apply to data kept by the State, the courts, counties, cities and state universities when used for noncommercial purposes the law allows (C.R.S. 6-1-1304(2)(o)). A state law also cannot bind a federal agency.

**What covers government instead:**

- **The Constitution.** The Fourth Amendment requires a warrant, in most cases, for a government search; the Fifth protects against being forced to testify against yourself (see "The Constitution", below). Courts have not yet said how either applies to data read from the brain.
- **The Privacy Act of 1974** governs records federal agencies keep about people: what they may collect, sharing, and a person's right to see and correct their record. It does not mention neural data.
- **Montana** is the one state we have found whose neural-data law limits government: since 1 October 2025, a government entity may obtain neural data only with a search warrant or investigative subpoena, and the data may not be stored in countries the United States sanctions.
- **Chile's** constitutional protection of brain activity binds the state as well as companies (see *Latin America*).

**When a foreign adversary is involved.**

- **The Justice Department's Data Security Program** (28 C.F.R. Part 202, in effect since 8 April 2025) prohibits U.S. companies from brokering Americans' bulk sensitive data to six countries of concern (China, Russia, Iran, North Korea, Cuba and Venezuela) and the people and companies tied to them, and restricts other deals, such as vendor, employment and investment agreements, that would give them access. It covers biometric, health, genomic, location and financial data and data linked to government employees. **Neural data is not named** among its categories.
- **Federal agencies must secure their systems** under the Federal Information Security Modernization Act, and cloud services they use must be authorised under FedRAMP. Neither names neural data.
- **Breaking into a computer system, stalking and harassment are federal crimes** whoever commits them, including on behalf of a foreign government: the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (18 U.S.C. § 1030) and the stalking law (18 U.S.C. § 2261A).

**What we have not found:** a law that names neural data held in a government system, sets security rules for it, or requires a person to be told if a foreign adversary gains access to it. If neural data is gathered in a government system, the rules that apply are the general ones above. Whether they are enough is one of the questions this page asks.

**On Colorado and the Justice Department:** we have found no change to the Colorado Privacy Act's neural-data protection since 2024, and no Justice Department action about it. The Justice Department's action in Colorado concerns the state's separate AI Act (see *Colorado: the AI Act and the federal challenge*).

- **Sources:** [C.R.S. 6-1-1304](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_6-1-1304); Department of Justice, [Privacy Act of 1974](https://www.justice.gov/opcl/privacy-act-1974); Cooley, ["Montana on the Brain: A Bold Step for Neural Privacy"](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-04-22-montana-on-the-brain-a-bold-step-for-neural-privacy) (22 April 2025); Department of Justice, [Data Security Program](https://www.justice.gov/nsd/data-security) and [the final rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/08/2024-31486/preventing-access-to-us-sensitive-personal-data-and-government-related-data-by-countries-of-concern) (Federal Register, 8 January 2025); Pillsbury, ["DOJ Data Security Program Compliance Guide"](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/doj-data-security-program-compliance-guide.html); CISA, [Federal Information Security Modernization Act](https://www.cisa.gov/topics/cyber-threats-and-advisories/federal-information-security-modernization-act); [FedRAMP](https://www.fedramp.gov); [18 U.S.C. § 1030](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1030); [18 U.S.C. § 2261A](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2261A).

## Neural data: federal proposal

- **MIND Act ([S. 2925](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/BILLS-119s2925is)):** introduced in the Senate in September 2025; would direct the Federal Trade Commission to study neural data for a year and recommend rules. Not enacted.

## Federal AI policy

These orders shape AI policy across the country. None of them is about neural data, and none creates rights a person can enforce.


### Executive Order 14365, "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence"
- **Status:** signed 11 December 2025.
- **What it does:** directs the Commerce Department to list state AI laws it considers burdensome, ties some broadband funding to that list, sets up a Justice Department AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state laws in court, asks the FCC and FTC to consider federal rules that would override state ones, and calls for federal legislation.
- **What it means for a person there:** state protections may be challenged in court or pressured through funding. They remain law unless a court or Congress says otherwise. The first such court action is in Colorado (below).
- **Analysis:** Nita Farahany ([*Thinking Freely*, 12 December 2025](https://nitafarahany.substack.com/p/the-executive-order-that-could-kill)) sets out the constitutional arguments on each side and notes that the Senate voted 99 to 1 in 2025 to remove a similar ten-year pause on state AI laws from the budget bill.
- **Sources:** [the order (govinfo.gov)](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/DCPD-202501186); Baker Botts, ["Inside the DOJ's New AI Litigation Task Force"](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102me4r/inside-the-dojs-new-ai-litigation-task-force).

### Executive Order 14409, "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security"
- **Status:** signed 2 June 2026; published in the Federal Register on 5 June 2026.
- **What it covers:** security testing and coordination for the most capable AI models, including risks related to cyberattacks and to chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear weapons.
- **Analysis:** Nita Farahany ([*Thinking Freely*, 26 October 2026](https://nitafarahany.substack.com/p/chemical-biological-radiological)) describes the testing as voluntary and argues that every proposed safeguard against AI-assisted weapons leads back to more surveillance, first of AI models and then of people.
- **Sources:** [Federal Register document 2026-11415](https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2026-11415).

### Executive order "Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence"
- **Status:** signed 29 September 2026; Federal Register document 2026-20321.
- **What it does:** directs the executive branch to use "Super Intelligence" and "SI" in place of "Artificial Intelligence" and "AI". A voluntary "White House Accord on Super Intelligence" was signed at the same time with leaders of AI companies.
- **Analysis:** Nita Farahany ([*Thinking Freely*, 2 October 2026](https://nitafarahany.substack.com/p/a-bonus-class-on-the-two-executive)): neither this order nor California's response the next day creates enforceable rights.
- **Sources:** whitehouse.gov: [the order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/09/inaugurating-the-era-of-super-intelligence/) and [its fact sheet](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/09/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-inaugurates-the-era-of-super-intelligence/); [Federal Register document 2026-20321](https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2026-20321).

## The Constitution: thought and the Fifth Amendment

The Fifth Amendment protects a person from being forced to testify against themselves: what comes from the mind, not physical evidence such as blood or fingerprints. Nita Farahany ([*Thinking Freely*, 30 March 2026](https://nitafarahany.substack.com/p/the-government-can-take-your-blood)) explains why devices that read intention before a person moves, and AI conversation logs that record thinking in progress, strain that line, and that the courts have not settled it. Cases: *Schmerber v. California* (1966), *Fisher v. United States* (1976), *United States v. Hubbell* (2000).

## Colorado: the AI Act and the federal challenge

### Automated Decision-Making Technology Act (SB 26-189)
- **Status:** signed 14 May 2026; takes effect 1 January 2027 if the Attorney General completes the required rules. It replaced Colorado's 2024 AI Act (SB 24-205) before that law took effect.
- **What it covers:** telling people when automated technology is used in important decisions about them; a right to ask for meaningful human review after a decision goes against them; disclosures within 30 days. It drops the 2024 law's duty to take reasonable care against algorithmic discrimination.
- **The federal challenge:** an AI company sued in federal court in Denver on 9 April 2026 to block the 2024 AI Act, and on 24 April 2026 the Justice Department joined with its own complaint, arguing the law's rules on algorithmic discrimination violate equal protection. This is the first court action by the Justice Department's AI Litigation Task Force against a state AI law (Executive Order 14365, above). On 27 April the company and the Attorney General agreed to pause enforcement while the case and the rewrite went ahead; both are expected to challenge the replacement law too. **The case is about the AI Act, not the Colorado Privacy Act: neural data and brain-computer interfaces are not part of it.** Nita Farahany had named Colorado's AI law as a likely target in December 2025 ([*Thinking Freely*](https://nitafarahany.substack.com/p/the-executive-order-that-could-kill)).
- **Enforcement:** the Attorney General only; up to $20,000 per violation, with 60 days to fix a problem first.
- **Sources:** Jenner & Block, ["DOJ Joins xAI in Lawsuit Challenging Colorado AI Act"](https://www.jenner.com/en/news-insights/client-alerts/doj-joins-xai-in-lawsuit-challenging-colorado-ai-act); McDermott Will & Schulte, ["Colorado AI law in flux"](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/colorado-ai-law-in-flux-comprehensive-replacement-bill-signed-after-federal-court-blocks-predecessors-enforcement/); Skadden, ["Colorado Repeals and Replaces Its AI Act"](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/06/colorado-repeals-and-replaces-its-ai-act) (June 2026).

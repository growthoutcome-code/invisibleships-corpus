# The law: European Union

## The AI Act, as amended in 2026

- **Status:** in force since 1 August 2024 and applied in stages. Its bans on certain AI practices have applied since 2 February 2025. Most transparency duties apply from 2 August 2026.
- **The 2026 amendment:** Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, published 24 July 2026 and in force from 27 July 2026, moved the duties for high-risk AI. They now apply from **2 December 2027** for stand-alone systems such as those used in hiring, credit and education, and from **2 August 2028** for AI built into regulated products such as medical devices. It also added bans on AI that generates child sexual abuse material and on "nudifier" apps.
- **What it covers:** banned practices; heavy duties for high-risk systems; transparency for chatbots and synthetic media; rules for general-purpose AI models, supervised by the EU's AI Office.
- **What it means for a person there:** some AI uses on people are banned outright. Many protections for high-risk uses now start in late 2027 or 2028.
- **What it does not cover:** AI used only for military, defence or national security; it does not create a general right to mental privacy.
- **Analysis:** Nita Farahany ([*Thinking Freely*, 12 November 2025](https://nitafarahany.substack.com/p/understanding-how-the-eu-regulates)) describes one AI system falling under six or more EU laws at once, including the GDPR, the Data Act, the Digital Services Act and cybersecurity rules, with overlapping impact assessments and incident reports due to different authorities on different deadlines.
- **Sources:** [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) and [Regulation (EU) 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj) (EUR-Lex); White & Case, ["EU AI Omnibus enters into force"](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/eu-ai-omnibus-enters-force-amending-ai-act) (July 2026).

## Germany: reform of the intelligence services law (2026)

- **Status:** passed by the Bundestag on 13 November 2026 (bill 21/7868, in the Interior Committee's version), by 308 votes to 190 with 71 abstentions. It still goes to the Bundesrat; the date of entry into force is not yet known.
- **What it covers:** according to the Bundestag, it gives the domestic and foreign intelligence services legal bases to compare image data biometrically and to use artificial intelligence in their analysis, widens their cyber powers (including access to attackers' IT systems under narrow conditions), and moves prior review and data-protection oversight to an independent control council.
- **What it means for a person there:** the parliament's own summary records that opposition parties objected to biometric matching against internet data without adequate thresholds.
- **Sources:** Deutscher Bundestag, ["Bundestag reformiert das Nachrichtendienstrecht"](https://www.bundestag.de/dokumente/textarchiv/2026/kw46-de-nachrichtendienste-1231530) (13 November 2026).

## Italy: consent before an app reads a phone's contents (enforcement, 2026)

- **What happened:** on 14 November 2026 Italy's data-protection authority (the Garante) announced a fine of €805 million on Meta Platforms Ireland. It says the Facebook app for Android read photos, videos and their metadata on about 20 million Italian users' phones through a gallery-suggestion feature switched on by default since 2023, without specific consent or adequate information.
- **The rule applied:** the Italian privacy code's provision implementing the EU ePrivacy Directive, which the authority says protects the phone as part of a person's private sphere and requires consent for any access to it. The authority rejected the argument that only the Irish authority could decide the case.
- **What it means for a person there:** the authority ordered the company to ask for separate consent within 60 days and to keep the feature off by default. The company can challenge the decision.
- **Sources:** Garante per la protezione dei dati personali, [press release](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/web/guest/home/docweb/-/docweb-display/docweb/10321080) (14 November 2026).

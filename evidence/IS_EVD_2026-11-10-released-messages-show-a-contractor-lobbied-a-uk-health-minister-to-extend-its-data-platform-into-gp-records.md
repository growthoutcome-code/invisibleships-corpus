# Palantir lobbied Scottish Labour MP to extend reach of AI in NHS

**PALANTIR lobbied a Scottish Labour MP to consider extending its AI technology into GP records in England, according to reports.**

The [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/09/revealed-palantir-lobbied-minister-to-stretch-ai-tools-reach-to-nhs-gp-records?ref=ed_recent) has revealed that former health minister Zubir Ahmed – Labour MP for Glasgow South West – was personally lobbied by the controversial US surveillance firm.

Palantir, co-founded by Trump-supporting billionaire Peter Thiel, has been used by the US [Immigration and Customs Enforcement agency (ICE)](https://www.thenational.scot/news/25803237.us-representative-targeted-public-event-minneapolis/?ref=ed_latest) to target, identify and locate immigrants.

Billionaire Donald Trump donor Peter Thiel co-founded Palantir *(Image: PA)*

The firm has also been accused of selling its services to Israel's IDF and has been identified as "profiting from genocide" in [Gaza,](https://www.thenational.scot/topics/Gaza/?ref=au) according to Francesca Albanese, UN special rapporteur on human rights in the Occupied Palestinian Territories.

Palantir [was previously accused of "intimidation"](https://www-tmp.thenational.scot/news/26661288.us-spy-tech-firm-palantir-intimidating-mps-speak-it/?ref=ed_direct) after writing letters to MPs who raised concerns about its involvement with the [UK Government](https://www.thenational.scot/topics/uk-government/?ref=au).

The Guardian reports that Palantir used a direct WhatsApp channel to argue that "the logic of joining up primary and secondary \[care\] data is unarguable" and that its software was "ideal" for the job.

**READ MORE: US aircraft moved to Prestwick airport after RAF base receives 'Iran threat'**

The push came in February this year, despite GP data being explicitly ruled out of the scope of Palantir's [existing £330 million contract with NHS England](https://www.thenational.scot/news/25932765.nhs-england-urged-axe-contract-palantir/?ref=ed_direct).

The messages between Ahmed and Palantir's UK boss, Louis Mosley, span several months from July 2025 to March 2026.

Ahmed [quit as health ministe](https://www.thenational.scot/news/26097894.live-updates-keir-starmer-fights-stay-prime-minister/?ref=ed_direct)r just two months later, in May, as part of efforts to force [Keir Starmer](https://www.thenational.scot/topics/keir-starmer/?ref=au)'s resignation as prime minister.

In the messages and a briefing document, Palantir urged Ahmed to "get out ahead" of likely "political" opposition to Palantir, such as from Green Party of England and Wales leader Zack Polanski and Your Party, led by [Jeremy Corbyn](https://www.thenational.scot/topics/jeremy-corbyn/?ref=au) and Zarah Sultana.

The company also told Ahmed that the existing NHS England contract is "already delivering very strong results" but "will deliver even more [benefits](https://www.thenational.scot/news/25063718.dwp-news-interviews-updates/?ref=au) faster with the right ministerial sponsorship".

Meanwhile, the messages also appear to show Ahmed praising [Mosley's performance on the BBC's flagship Sunday politics programme](https://www.thenational.scot/news/25759699.palantir-uk-boss-louis-mosley/?ref=ed_direct) just after he had appeared, telling him: "Great work \[on\] Kuenssberg btw".

The messages were obtained under Freedom of Information (FOI) by tech equity campaign group Foxglove and shared with The Guardian.

The lobbying does not relate to [NHS Scotland](https://www.thenational.scot/topics/nhs-scotland/?ref=au), as health north of the Border is devolved from [Westminster](https://www.thenational.scot/topics/westminster/?ref=au).

The [Scottish Government](https://www.thenational.scot/topics/scottish-government/?ref=au) has repeatedly made clear [that it will not work with Palantir,](https://www-tmp.thenational.scot/news/26699423.snp-urged-keep-technofascist-palantir-public-sector/?ref=ed_direct) with a spokesperson previously saying: "Our NHS will never be for sale."

Commenting, the [SNP](https://www.thenational.scot/news/25059069.news-interviews-updates-snp/?ref=au) said the Labour Government has "serious questions to answer".

SNP health spokesperson Seamus Logan said: "Why is a [Scottish Labour](https://www.thenational.scot/topics/scottish-labour/?ref=au) MP sending sycophantic messages to a Palantir chief and do they support their disturbing plans to access sensitive GP records?

"Michael Marra has an issue on his hands – his MPs clearly want to open up NHS Scotland to Palantir, so where does he stand? The Scottish public deserve to know."

Logan continued: "The SNP has been unequivocal that NHS Scotland and patient data will never be opened up to the likes of Palantir and it is now essential for transparency that the UK Labour Government confirms the extent of the lobbying by publishing all sneaky WhatsApp messages held between ministers and Palantir.

"Through a fresh start with independence, our NHS will never be exposed to the firms like Palantir through the soft underbelly of Westminster."

A Palantir spokesperson said: "We've long argued that data sharing can improve the NHS, for patients and taxpayers, and that our software helps different NHS organisations to do that, while ensuring they maintain full local control over their data.

"We've also always been clear that it is for the government to decide how they roll the software out. These exchanges reflect both of those points."

**READ MORE: 'Beneath contempt': Ed Miliband condemned over 'performative' Gaza video**

Meanwhile, Marc Farr, the chair of the NHS Chief Data and Analytical Officer Network, told The Guardian: "Palantir appears to be bidding to stretch the scope of its work for NHS England in a way that goes beyond statements from ministers and NHS leaders that GP data was not in the scope of the FDP contract and would require legislative change.

"As two separate select committees have noted, public trust and transparency is essential to us being able to realise the fantastic opportunities that technology and data offer."

The Department for [Health](https://www.thenational.scot/news/health/?ref=au) and Social Care declined to comment when approached by The Guardian.

## Captured text

A **new scientific publication** from the **[META-BRAIN project](https://meta-brain.eu/the-project/)** has been published in ***[Advanced Science](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/)***, presenting **experimental evidence** that magnetoelectric nanoparticles can enable the modulation of cortical neuronal networks using **low-intensity static magnetic fields** that, on their own, did not alter neuronal activity in the experimental model. The findings represent an **important step** towards future **wireless and minimally invasive neuromodulation technologies**.

The study, entitled **“*[Magnetoelectric Nanoparticles Enable Modulation of Cortical Networks by Low-Intensity Static Magnetic Fields In Vitro](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.76867)*“**, provides **strong experimental support** for one of META-BRAIN’s key research objectives: exploring **how magnetoelectric nanoparticles** can **convert** otherwise biologically ineffective magnetic fields  capable of **modulating neuronal network activity**. The work was published in ***Advanced Science***, one of the **leading international interdisciplinary journals** in advanced materials, engineering and the life sciences.

Achieving precise control of brain activity remains one of the **major challenges in neuroscience**. Although several non-invasive brain stimulation techniques are already available, they still **face important limitations**, particularly regarding their ability to precisely target specific brain regions. To overcome these limitations, the META-BRAIN project is investigating **innovative approaches** based on **magnetoelectric nanoparticles and ultrasound technologies**, with the long-term goal of developing more precise and less invasive strategies for modulating brain activity.

## **A step towards more precise brain stimulation**

The study focused on magnetoelectric nanoparticles, one of the project’s core research areas. These specially engineered nanoparticles are designed to **convert magnetic stimulation into local electrical signals** by means of their core-shell interactions, making them **capable of interacting with nearby neurons**.

To investigate their potential, the researchers combined nanoparticle fabrication, computational modelling and electrophysiology experiments measuring neuronal activity. The study showed that **low-intensity static magnetic fields**, which by themselves did not modify neuronal activity, **became effective** neuromodulation stimuli in the presence of magnetoelectric nanoparticles. Under these conditions, **neuronal networks became more active**, showing increased excitability and a higher frequency of spontaneous rhythmic activity.

According to the authors, this approach could open **new possibilities for highly targeted**, **wireless neuromodulation strategies** in the future.

## **International collaboration across the META-BRAIN consortium**

The study was carried out by researchers from META-BRAIN partners **IDIBAPS** (Barcelona), **ETH Zurich**, **CIBER**, represented by the **Institute of Microelectronics of Barcelona (IMB-CNM, CSIC)** and **CNR-IEEIT** (Milan). The authors of the study are [Nathalia Cancino-Fuentes](https://meta-brain.eu/news/nathalia-cancino-impact-brain-modulation/), Alejandro Suarez-Perez, Elric Zhang, Hao Ye, [Marta Bonato](https://meta-brain.eu/news/marta-bonato-computational-models/), [Joana Covelo](https://meta-brain.eu/news/joana-covelo-meta-brain-taking-risks/), Vitaly Pustovalov, Anton Guimerà-Brunet, Xavi Illa, Valentin Gantenbein, Marta Parazzini, Salvador Pané, Cagatay M. Oral and Maria V. Sanchez-Vives.

Commenting on the significance of the findings, **Prof. Sanchez-Vives**, Scientific Coordinator of META-BRAIN and senior author of the study, said:

“*This study **demonstrates** that magnetoelectric nanoparticles can modulate the dynamics of cortical networks. Although this is still an in vitro demonstration, it **opens an exciting pathway** towards highly targeted, wireless neuromodulation strategies with the **potential to reach deep brain regions** while reducing the need for more invasive approaches. **Translation for clinical use is always on our horizon**”.*


The publication is **available** in *Advanced Science* here: [https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.76867](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.76867)

*This is the online edition of The Wiretap newsletter, your weekly digest of cybersecurity, internet privacy and surveillance news. To get it in your inbox, subscribe here.*

Under the second Trump administration, **ICE has spent more on cloud services from Amazon and Microsoft than ever before**, according to federal contracting records. Between March and November last year, ICE ordered $38 million in Microsoft software via reselling partners like Dell Federal Systems. The same agency made a $25 million purchase of Amazon Web Services’ cloud systems in September. That month, ICE also spent $530,000 on Google cloud products.

That doesn’t include deals with Customs and Border Protection, whose resources have shifted to assisting ICE in the White House-mandated crackdown on immigration across the U.S. Also through Dell, Customs and Border Protection purchased $38 million in Microsoft licenses in March, as well as nearly $39 million worth of AWS cloud services — the latter a record for Amazon.

ICE’s remit goes beyond immigration enforcement and also includes narcotics and child exploitation. The technologies it’s bought from Amazon and Microsoft, which are for cloud computing and productivity tools, are not explicitly for the agency’s surveillance or enforcement operations.

But some of the tech giants’ staff are displeased with their employers’ ICE deals. After ICE agents shot and killed ICU nurse Alex Pretti this month in Minneapolis, hundreds of tech workers—including over 100 from Amazon, Google and Microsoft—have joined a campaign called ICEout.tech, calling on their paymasters to cancel ICE contracts. The letter also asked that CEOs call the White House and “demand that ICE leave our cities.” The campaign’s website was set up on January 9, two days after an ICE agent shot and killed Renee Good, also in Minneapolis.

Amazon, Google and Microsoft did not respond to a comment request. None of the company’s CEOs have spoken out on the matter. On the day Pretti was killed, a number of tech chiefs [reportedly](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/01/26/business/alex-pretti-melania-trump-corporate-ceos) attended a private White House screening of the Melania documentary produced by Amazon MGM Studios. Amazon CEO Andy Jassy was among them.

As *Forbes* reported on Monday, Palantir is one of ICE’s biggest suppliers and is the recipient of its [biggest contract, at near $140 million](https://www.forbes.com/sites/tylerroush/2026/01/26/these-companies-palantir-att-deloitte-have-the-biggest-ice-contracts-as-dhs-funding-under-fire/), signed in 2022. Per a [*Wired*](https://www.wired.com/story/palantir-ice-dhs-alex-pretti-killing-workers-slack-minneapolis/) report, some employees inside the Peter Thiel-founded firm have voiced concerns about its contracts with ICE, which includes over $60 million for surveillance tools since Trump returned to the White House, including software that assists in identifying targets for deportation. Staff took to Slack to vent their frustrations at being associated with the crackdown, with one saying “In my opinion ICE are the bad guys.” Palantir’s leadership responded, telling employees the company was helping better inform ICE enforcement.

Though ICE contracting tech giants have remained quiet in the face of the killings, some tech luminaries have spoken up. LinkedIn cofounder Reid Hoffman, who is on Microsoft’s board, retweeted a post from billionaire VC investor Vinod Khosla, which bemoaned “macho ICE vigilantes running amuck empowered by a conscious-less administration.” Hoffman reiterated what Khosla wrote: “There is politics but humanity should transcend that.”

## THE BIG STORY:

## Microsoft Gave FBI Keys To Unlock Encrypted Data

In an unprecedented case, [Microsoft provided the FBI with keys](https://www.forbes.com/sites/thomasbrewster/2026/01/22/microsoft-gave-fbi-keys-to-unlock-bitlocker-encrypted-data/) to unlock data on three laptops protected by BitLocker encryption. Microsoft told *Forbes* it receives around **20 requests for BitLocker keys every year**. In many, but not all cases the tech giant said the keys aren’t stored in its cloud servers so it can’t assist. Privacy advocates said Microsoft could encrypt keys even when they’re in the cloud — as Apple does.

## Captured text

Defence is set to spend at least $100 million annually on Microsoft Azure services until 2030.

Under a new five-year agreement, starting on September 1, Defence has committed to consume [$495 million](https://www.tenders.gov.au/Cn/Show/fd6ad783-7c68-4d6c-ba3c-b4d099f3a3a5) of Azure-based services.

This latest contract appears to replace a [previous three-year arrangement,](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/defence-dewr-drop-160m-on-microsoft-software-azure-583610) which expired on June 30 and was worth [$107 million.](https://www.tenders.gov.au/Cn/Show/630d6fbb-4035-46c4-ac65-dd8d7e4f1fae)

Like its apparent predecessor, the new agreement was secured through the government’s exclusive Microsoft licence reseller Data#3, via the Software and ERP Marketplace Panel.

The agreement shows that while Defence has committed over [$2 billion to rival Amazon Web Services (AWS)](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/australian-government-signs-aws-to-deliver-2bn-top-secret-cloud-609426?utm_source=chatgpt.com) for a classified cloud service supporting the Australian Signals Directorate (ASD), it still plans to host some significant enterprise workloads in Microsoft Azure.

Primarily, Azure will support Defence’s in-progress [SAP-based Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) platform,](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/defence-turns-to-microsoft-to-host-sap-erp-system-549377) which recently went live with modules for logistics, maintenance, finance and procurement.

In tandem, Defence has [a Microsoft 365 environment known as Vera that is also being expanded.](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/defence-to-expand-its-vera-m365-environment-612655)

Defence CIO Chris Crozier [outlined his ambition](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/defence-to-expand-its-vera-m365-environment-612655) to transition to more cloud-based productivity, unified communications, and collaboration tooling in the M365 ecosystem late last year.

Speaking on the *iTnews Podcast* last year, Crozier also expressed interest in adopting Microsoft Copilot, but admitted Defence’s current environment is “not \[yet\] architected” for the tool.

Meanwhile, in the past week, Defence committed to additional major technology contracts, including [$59 million for Citrix](https://www.tenders.gov.au/Cn/Show/8af24338-bda7-42a4-be88-2a0e1eba2bda) services over three years and [$11 million](https://www.tenders.gov.au/Cn/Show/2557795e-266e-4f56-9936-d14e9a618637) for a one-year deal with Oracle.

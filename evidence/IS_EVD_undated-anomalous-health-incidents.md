### [Patient Registry Factsheet](https://www.health.mil/Reference-Center/Fact-Sheets/2025/03/10/Patient-Registry-Factsheet)

The Anomalous Health Incidents Patient Registry collects patient information after an AHI event.

Accessing Military Health Care During Lapse in Appropriations

The Military Health System will continue to provide health care during a government lapse in appropriations. There may be some effects on the delivery of health care services within military hospitals and clinics. Contact your hospital or clinic to confirm hours of operations and services provided.

The Department of Defense and Defense Health Agency are committed to preventing, treatment, and recovering patients who report symptoms of an Anomalous Health Incident. DHA is actively developing a standard care pathway for both DOD and non-DOD beneficiaries to receive appropriate medical care as quickly as possible. To support this, patient resources have been developed to provide information on how to seek medical attention within the Military Health System and an overview of medical care coverage. Additionally, multiple provider resources are available to support care management and ensure proper documentation of treatment. This site will continue to be updated with products, trainings, and resources as they become available.

Previously referred to as Havanna Syndrome or as an Unconventional Brain Injury, AHI is the term used to describe a group of sudden symptoms that occur after an unexplained sensory event or events that are not associated with a history of head trauma. AHI events have been reported by several DOD and Federal Agency personnel since 2016.

Possible symptoms that are associated with an AHI event may include but are not limited to:

- Auditory Symptoms
- Balance Problems
- Cognitive complaints
- Difficulty concentrating
- Head Pressure
- Headache
- Hearing Changes
- Memory Problems
- Nausea
- Dizziness

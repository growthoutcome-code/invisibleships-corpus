## Captured text

The U.N. [warned](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/08/world/europe/united-nations-technology-warning.html) Thursday that rapid advances in neurotechnology could one day put fundamental human rights at risk. As new and emerging tools to monitor or moderate neural activity expand beyond the medical sector, governments could gain unprecedented access to people’s minds for use in criminal investigations and court proceedings. That led U.N. Secretary-General Antonio Guterres to caution in a new report that the technology “raises profound human rights challenges when applied to the administration of justice.”

The report recommended stronger legal safeguards, including classifying thoughts as “highly sensitive personal data” and requiring explicit consent before it can be used in criminal cases.

As Roland Benedikter [wrote for WPR](https://www.worldpoliticsreview.com/neurotechnology-regulations-ethics-technology/) last year, “there are currently no binding rules on neurotechnology research and applications,” making the report an important first step toward raising global awareness of these risks.

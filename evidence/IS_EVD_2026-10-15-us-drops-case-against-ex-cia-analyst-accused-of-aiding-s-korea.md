# US drops case against ex-CIA analyst accused of aiding S. Korea

## Summary

U.S. prosecutors agreed to drop charges against former CIA analyst Sue Mi Terry in New York after reaching a deferred prosecution agreement Tuesday. Terry was accused of acting as an unregistered agent for South Korea and disclosing U.S. government information. She will avoid jail if she follows restrictions on travel and contact with South Korean intelligence for 12 months.

## Key Facts

- Terry was charged in 2024 with failing to register as a foreign agent and disclosing U.S. government information to South Korean intelligence.
- The indictment alleged that South Korean handlers gave Terry a $3,450 Louis Vuitton handbag, a $2,950 Bottega Veneta handbag and a $2,845 Dolce & Gabbana coat.
- The alleged benefits also included visits to multiple Michelin-starred restaurants and $37,000 in covert payments to a think tank where Terry worked.
- Terry is a naturalized U.S. citizen born in Seoul who worked for the U.S. government from 2001 to around 2011.

**AFP**

NEW YORK — U.S. prosecutors have agreed to drop charges against a former CIA analyst accused of working as an agent of the South Korean government in exchange for designer handbags, high-priced dinners and other luxury goods.

Sue Mi Terry, who had also served on the White House National Security Council, was charged in 2024 for failing to register as a foreign agent and disclosing U.S. government information to South Korean intelligence.

A court filing Tuesday showed the Justice Department has struck a deferred prosecution agreement with Terry, who denied the accusations and was due to go on trial later this year.

It means Terry, previously a senior fellow for Korea studies at the Council on Foreign Relations, will not face jail time provided she complies with several rules for the next 12 months.

They include restrictions on leaving the United States, and a ban on communicating with the South Korean intelligence service.

Terry is a naturalized U.S. citizen who was born in South Korea's capital, Seoul, and raised in Virginia and Hawaii.

She was employed by the U.S. government from 2001 to around 2011, and was allegedly to have begun her work for South Korea in 2013.

In return for her services, Terry's South Korean handlers gifted her a $3,450 Louis Vuitton handbag, a $2,950 Bottega Veneta handbag, and a $2,845 Dolce & Gabbana coat, according to an indictment.

She was also taken to "multiple" Michelin-starred restaurants and provided with $37,000 in covert payments to a think tank where she worked.

Her lawyer, Lee Wolosky, said in a statement carried by U.S. media Tuesday that Terry had served her country "loyally for many years."

"We have consistently maintained that her interactions with South Korean officials were conducted in furtherance of U.S. interests and her work as a leading American specialist on North Korea and East Asia," Wolosky said.

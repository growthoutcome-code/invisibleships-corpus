# Critical Information Infrastructure of Russia

Critical Information Infrastructure (CII) is a set of information systems and telecommunication networks that are critical for the work of key areas of the state and society: health care, industry, communications, transport, power, the financial sector and urban economy. This article discusses regulatory and practical aspects related to CII in Russia.

### The government introduces new deadlines for the transfer of significant objects of CII to Russian software

In mid-May 2026, it became known that [the Ministry of Digital Development](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Ministry_of_Digital_Development) of the Russian Federation had determined new terms for the transfer of significant objects of critical information infrastructure (CII) to Russian software. In some cases, the limit date is set on January 1, 2036.

According to [Interfax](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Interfax), the Ministry of Ministry of Digital Development says that in general, exclusively domestic software should be used at significant KII facilities from January 1, 2028. For situations when a particularly significant project was implemented at a typical CII facility by January 1, 2026, or if a contract for the development of the relevant Russian software was concluded for a specific significant CII facility by September 1, 2027, other transition dates are established - up to January 1, 2031. And for those cases when, during 2026-2027, especially significant projects were launched in relation to typical KII facilities, the deadline was set on January 1, 2036.

The specified time frame will also apply to significant objects of CII, created respectively before January 1, 2026 and until January 1, 2028 - provided that there is no necessary domestic software for these objects. At the same time, it is proposed to take five years from the date of entry into force of the change in the list of relevant objects to transfer standard KII objects created after January 1, 2027 to Russian software.

The document also says that in order to organize the transfer of significant KII facilities to Russian program solutions, federal ministries and departments, the [Bank of Russia](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Bank_of_Russia), the state corporation [Roscosmos](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Roskosmos) and [Rosatom](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Rosatom) must approve industry plans by September 1, 2026. In these structures, the officials (at least the deputy head) responsible for organizing the transition will be identified.[\[1\]](https://tadviser.com#cite_note-0)

### Gazprom and Transneft warn: requirements for the use of trusted PACS at KII facilities will create problems for business

The government's requirements for the use of trusted hardware and software systems (PAC) at significant facilities [of the critical information infrastructure](https://tadviser.com/index.php/CUES) ([CII)](https://tadviser.com/index.php/CUES), according to market participants, may turn into problems for business. Gazprom [and](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Gazprom) [Transneft](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Transneft) warned about this in mid-March 2026.

Until 2030, all KII facilities in [Russia](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Russia) should switch to trusted PACS. Such complexes, according to Forbes, must meet three criteria: enter the register of Russian radio-electronic products, use [the software](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Software) in accordance with the requirements of state customers and have a document or certificate from [the FSTEC](<https://tadviser.com/index.php/Company:Federal_Service_for_Technical_and_Export_Control_(FSTEC_of_Russia)>) or [the FSB confirming](https://tadviser.com/index.php/FSB) compliance with the requirements of these departments "if the information protection function is implemented." And it is the third point that causes concern among industry participants: the rules impose on companies the obligation to additionally certify PACs with information protection function, even when this function is not the main one (for example, password login).

[Gazprom](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Gazprom)and Transneft declare that the mandatory use of certified hardware and software systems on critical information infrastructures will negatively affect the activities of companies

Sergey Gokhshtein, Deputy Director of PCS Department, says that in accordance with these criteria, almost any equipment used at significant CII facilities will have to use certified funds. At the same time, according to him, it may take one and a half years and several million [rubles](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Russian_ruble) to obtain only one certificate.

The company proposes to "not introduce mandatory certification where it is not required."[\[3\]](https://tadviser.com#cite_note-2)

### Russia adopted a law on criminal prosecution for technical errors in the operation of CII

On January 27, 2026, the State Duma of the [Russian Federation](https://tadviser.com/index.php/State_Duma_of_the_Russian_Federation) adopted bills that establish a two-level system of responsibility for incidents in the field of [critical information infrastructure](https://tadviser.com/index.php/CUES) ([CII).](https://tadviser.com/index.php/CUES) In particular, in case of violations that entailed damage, criminal liability is provided.

According to the Kommersant newspaper, one of the bills amends Art. 274 of the Criminal Code of [the](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Company:ID_Kommersant) Russian Federation ("Violation of the rules for the operation of means of storing, processing or transmitting computer information and information and [telecommunication](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Telecommunications) networks"), while the second contains a new article of the Administrative Code. Criminal liability threatens for proven destruction, blocking, modification or copying of CII data. Administrative punishment is introduced for violations of the rules of operation without such consequences.

[of](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Russia)CII

The document defines the amount of fines. For citizens, they will amount to 5-10 thousand [rubles](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Russian_ruble), for officials - 10-50 thousand rubles, for companies - 100-500 thousand rubles. At the same time, an employee of the organization who committed a violation can be completely exempted from criminal liability for him if he retains evidence and actively cooperates with the investigation.

The purpose of the bill, according to its authors, is to "protect ordinary specialists from criminal prosecution for technical errors." However, as Anton Eremenko, a consultant to the consulting and audit department of [Informzaschita](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Company:Informzaschita), notes, the introduction of a two-level responsibility system can create the risk of substitution of system problems with personal errors. At the same time, the provision on voluntary cooperation leads to a conflict of interest: an employee seeking to minimize personal risks can cooperate with the investigation to the detriment of the interests of a company that prefers internal proceedings.[\[5\]](https://tadviser.com#cite_note-4)

### KII facilities received a deferral for the transition to Russian software and hardware complexes, but not all

In mid-December 2025, the Ministry of [Industry and Trade](<https://tadviser.com/index.php?title=Ministry_of_Industry_and_Trade_of_the_Russian_Federation_(Ministry_of_Industry_and_Trade)&action=edit&redlink=1>) published a draft document "On Amendments to the Decree of the Government [of the Russian Federation](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Company:Government_of_the_Russian_Federation) of November 14, 2023 No. 1912." We are talking about the introduction of provisions allowing the subjects of [the critical information infrastructure](https://tadviser.com/index.php/CUES) (CII) to extend the transition to the preferential use of trusted software [and](https://tadviser.com/index.php/CUES) hardware systems (PAC) at significant objects belonging to them beyond 2030.

The published document says that from January 1, 2030, it is not allowed for KII subjects to use PACS sites that are not trusted at their sites. At the same time, a number of exceptions are provided - it will be possible to extend the transition dates in the following cases:

- If there is a contract for the supply of a trusted PAC that extends beyond 2030;
- Prior to the expiry of the useful life or write-off of the PAC;
- If it is impossible to stop the work (according to production, technical and technological conditions) of the production facility where the PAC operates;
- Due to the inseparability of PAC from the capital construction facility.


In addition, a rule is provided according to which PAC is recognized as trusted before the expiration of its useful life, which eliminates the risks of the need to replace equipment if the manufacturer of such PAC has not repeatedly confirmed the Russian origin of the equipment or [software](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Software). At the same time, the criteria for recognizing PAC as a trusted person are supplemented by the possibility of confirming the power of attorney by checking the presence of information about the software and hardware used in its composition in the register of Russian industrial products. Similarly, the criterion for equipment performing [information protection](https://tadviser.com/index.php?title=Information_protection&action=edit&redlink=1) functions has been finalized.[\[6\]](https://tadviser.com#cite_note-5)

### FSTEC: 1,100 violations revealed at KII facilities

In November 2025, regulatory and regulatory bodies identified more than 1,100 security violations [critical information infrastructure](https://tadviser.com/index.php/CUES) (). [CUES](https://tadviser.com/index.php/CUES) [Russia](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Russia) These data were announced by [FSTEC](<https://tadviser.com/index.php/Company:Federal_Service_for_Technical_and_Export_Control_(FSTEC_of_Russia)>) Elena Torbenko, Head of the Federal Service for Technical and Export Control.

Torbenko said that 98% of violations are related to the discrepancy between the actual state of significant objects of the CII and the information entered in their official register. Among the problems, the head of FSTEC also considered insufficient control over the work of contractors with access to the information infrastructure of significant facilities.

Torbenko pointed to a continuing problem in which owners of significant objects do not search for and fix vulnerabilities in the software used. Additional typical violations, she said, are the lack of updates to antivirus databases and non-compliance with the rules for creating complex passwords and changing them regularly. Torbenko stressed that the management of companies should ensure that [information security](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Information_security) specialists comply with the established requirements.

The head of FSTEC paid special attention to the non-compliance of the owners of the KII facilities with presidential decree No. 250, which prohibits the use of [information protection](https://tadviser.com/index.php?title=Information_protection&action=edit&redlink=1) tools produced in unfriendly countries at significant facilities. The deadline for the implementation of this decree came on January 1, 2025. Torbenko said that the period for postponements is over, and administrative cases will be initiated for violation.

Critical information infrastructure includes information systems and automated management systems in key industries such as [healthcare](https://tadviser.com/index.php?title=Pharmaceuticals,_Medicine,_Healthcare&action=edit&redlink=1), transportation, power, [finance](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Finance) and industry. ### IT expenses of critical information infrastructure facilities in Russia reached ₽1,79 trillion over the year

The owners of critical [information infrastructure](https://tadviser.com/index.php/CUES) facilities in Russia in 2024 allocated ₽1,785 trillion for the purchase of domestic [IT solutions](https://tadviser.com/index.php/IT), which amounted to 51% of the total IT budgets of ₽3,5 trillion. These costs for the introduction of [information technologies](https://tadviser.com/index.php?title=Information_Technology&action=edit&redlink=1) at [CII](https://tadviser.com/index.php/CUES) facilities are given for the first time. Such calculations were presented by analysts [at iKS-Consulting](https://tadviser.com/index.php/IKS-Consulting) in August 2025.

According to [Sheets](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Company:Sheets)"," according to forecasts for 2025, the share of spending on CII solutions from the total amount of IT costs will remain at a similar level. From January 1, 2025, by decree of the president [Russia](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Russia) [Vladimir Putin](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Vladimir_Putin) , there is a ban on the use of a foreign one [software](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Software) at critical information infrastructure facilities.

20 sectors of the Russian economy are classified as CII, including the financial sector, telecommunications, mining and manufacturing, [healthcare](https://tadviser.com/index.php?title=Pharmaceuticals,_Medicine,_Healthcare&action=edit&redlink=1), social services, the military-industrial complex and other strategically important areas.

The cost structure of customers subject to CII regulatory requirements demonstrates priorities in the field of digitalization. The largest share - 21% of total IT costs - was software development and adaptation costs. 14% of funds were allocated for the purchase of finished [software](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Software), 8% for computing equipment, 7% for communication equipment, and 1% for software rental.

The main driver for the development of the entire Russian IT market was the dynamic [IT infrastructure](https://tadviser.com/index.php/IT_infrastructure) management segment. This is a class of solutions that allows you to adapt technical systems in real time: increase or reduce computing power, deploy backups, create dedicated environments and virtual machines to test new applications.

UDI software costs accounted for 5.3% of total IT customer spending in 2024, or ₽45,3 billion. Of this amount, ₽21,29 billion fell on solutions for critical information infrastructure facilities. The main consumers of UDI software were cloud providers, telecom operators, IT companies, large corporate customers from the enterprise segment and the public sector.

The dynamic IT infrastructure management segment is highly concentrated. About 74% of the market is controlled by seven companies: [Basis](<https://tadviser.com/index.php/Company:JV_Cloud_Platform_(Basis)>), [Cyberprotect](<https://tadviser.com/index.php/Company:Cyberprotect_(formerly_Acronis-Infozashchita,_Acronis-Infoprotect)>), [Orion soft](<https://tadviser.com/index.php?title=Company:Orion_soft_(Orion)&action=edit&redlink=1>), [Astra](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Company:Astra), [Flant](https://tadviser.com/index.php?title=Company:Flant&action=edit&redlink=1), [vStack](<https://tadviser.com/index.php/Company:VStack_(ITglobalcom_Labs)>) and [Dak M](https://tadviser.com/index.php?title=Dacom_M&action=edit&redlink=1). These players form the basis of the Russian market for infrastructure solutions for critical facilities.

Analysts predict different growth rates for individual segments of the IT market. If the software market as a whole grows by 11-12% annually, then by UDI class the growth will be 16.3% due to the expansion of the infrastructure and the scaling of the accumulated expertise in the field of import substitution.

Vitaly Popov, Director of the Infrastructure Projects Implementation Department of [Softline](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Company:Softline) Solutions, explained the market dynamics by changing customer approaches. According to him, if in 2022-2023 many companies were limited to pilot projects and testing solutions [for virtualization](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Virtualization), then in 2024 it became obvious that workstations alone were not enough to [provide business processes](https://tadviser.com/index.php/BPM).

An additional factor was the requirements of regulators for fault tolerance and reliability of information systems.

### FSTEC explains how to ensure the safety of remote work for employees of CII subjects

On March 26, 2020, [TAdviser](https://tadviser.com/index.php/TAdviser) became known that the [Federal Service for Technical and Export Control](https://tadviser.com/index.php/FSTEC) published a number of recommendations<sup>[\[59\]](https://tadviser.com#cite_note-58)</sup> entities to ensure the security of critical information infrastructure entities in the context<sup>[\[60\]](https://tadviser.com#cite_note-59)</sup> the [COVID-19 coronavirus](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Article:Coronavirus_COVID-19) pandemic. These recommendations are entirely related to the remote work of employees of organizations related to [CII](https://tadviser.com/index.php/CUES).

As rightly stated in the recommendations, remote access of workers to critical information infrastructure facilities creates some risks. In particular, there are threats of unauthorized access and influence on objects related to CII.

The FSTEC publication directly states that it is unacceptable to provide remote access in remote mode for any control over the operating modes of [industrial](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Industry) or technological equipment [of automated production and technological process control systems](https://tadviser.com/index.php/APCS) of critical [information infrastructure](https://tadviser.com/index.php/IT_infrastructure) facilities.

Otherwise, it is recommended to centrally allocate to employees leaving for "remote work" computer equipment through which they can access working [information](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Article:Information), having previously carried out their inventory, equipped with [antivirus](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Antivirus) tools and ensuring [the identification of](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Identification) their MAC addresses on the [servers](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Server) of CII objects.

Access to working resources from such devices should be implemented using the "white list" method and only through [VPN connections](https://tadviser.com/index.php/VPN). It is not recommended to provide employees with access from personal devices. Moreover, it is proposed to block the installation of foreign [software](https://tadviser.com/index.php?title=ON&action=edit&redlink=1) on operating SVTs.

FSTEC also advises to determine the list of [information](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Article:Information) and information resources (programs, volumes, directories, files) located on [the servers](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Server_platforms) of critical information infrastructure objects, to which, in principle, remote access will be provided and assign the minimum necessary rights and privileges to users leaving for remote work.

It is strongly recommended to exclude the possibility of use of remote computer equipment by unauthorized persons; implement [two-factor authentication to](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Two-factor_authentication) employees of remote SVTs so that one of the factors is provided by "a device separated from the critical information infrastructure object to which access is being made."

[Domain](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Article:Domains) names of fakes could differ from genuine ones by only one letter. How successful the attacks were, Cylance does not undertake to judge, since none of the attacked organizations is a client of the company. However, experts found that the campaign lasts at least three years.

Attackers first collect [data](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Article:Data) about specific persons using keyloggers, then conduct reconnaissance among the employees of this organization with whom the first victim contacts, and then try to lure new victims to their fake sites in order to collect additional information and access details.

The Big Four operators - [VimpelCom](https://tadviser.com/index.php/VimpelCom), [MegaFon](https://tadviser.com/index.php/MegaFon), [MTS](https://tadviser.com/index.php/MTS) and [Tele2](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Tele2) - sent an appeal to the head of the Ministry of [Communications](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Company:Ministry_of_Digital_Development,_Communications_and_Mass_Media) Nikolai Nikiforov. In it, they expressed their assessments of the bill on the protection of the critical infrastructure of the Runet, writes [Kommersant](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Kommersant) in October 2017.

The companies expressed fears that the document could deprive them of foreign shareholders with a share of more than 20%. They also reported that the information that 60% of Russian traffic passes through foreign exchange points is incorrect. According to their information, the real share is about 1%, since the cost of passing traffic through foreign routes is high. As a result, the authors of the letter are not clear why "traffic exchange points are assessed as critical infrastructure objects."

The Ministry of Telecom and Mass Communications told the publication that the ministry is conducting a "constructive dialogue" with operators on this issue. However, the Ministry of [Economic Development](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Ministry_of_Economic_Development) is also ready to speak with a negative response to the bill, where they said that the document is still undergoing a regulatory impact assessment.

### The Ministry of Finance criticized the bill on the critical infrastructure of the Runet

The Ministry of Finance sent a negative response to the bill of the Ministry [of](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Ministry_of_Finance_of_the_Russian_Federation) [Communications](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Company:Ministry_of_Digital_Development,_Communications_and_Mass_Media), which involves measures to ensure the safety of the critical infrastructure of the Russian segment of the [Internet](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Internet) in case of external impact. According [to RBC](https://tadviser.com/index.php/RBC), the main reason for the refusal was the lack of accurate information about the volumes and sources of financing.

"The presented financial and economic justification does not contain information about the amount of funds for the registration of \[domains\] and their part, planned on the GIS" Internet, "which does not allow to fully assess the costs of its creation and maintenance and assess the financial consequences of the adoption of the bill for the budgets of the budget system [of Russia](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Russia)," the text of the review says.

According to the publication, the Ministry of Finance proposed to finalize the bill by submitting a financial and economic justification. According to observers, the costs of implementing the initiative of the Ministry of Telecom and Mass Communications can amount to about 1.5 billion [rubles](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Russian_ruble), plus annual costs of about 40 million rubles.

Read more [here](https://tadviser.com/index.php?title=On_the_Security_of_the_Critical_Information_Infrastructure_of_the_Russian_Federation&action=edit&redlink=1).

### Federation Council approves criminal liability for attacks on critical IT infrastructure

On July 19, it became known that [Federation Council](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Federation_Council_of_the_Federal_Assembly_of_the_Russian_Federation) it approved the law "On Security," [critical information infrastructure](https://tadviser.com/index.php/CUES) developed [Federal Security Service (FSB)](<https://tadviser.com/index.php/Company:Federal_Security_Service_(FSB_of_the_Russian_Federation)>) and introduced [State Duma](https://tadviser.com/index.php/State_Duma) [Government](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Company:Government_of_the_Russian_Federation) in December 2016. The document will enter into force from the beginning of 2018[\[70\]](https://tadviser.com#cite_note-69)

The law introduces the classification of objects of critical information infrastructure and provides for the creation of a register of such objects, while determining the rights and obligations of both owners of objects and the bodies that protect these objects. The body, which will be responsible for ensuring the safety of infrastructure, has not yet been appointed.

The document also provides for the creation of a [state](https://tadviser.com/index.php/State) system for the detection, prevention and elimination of the consequences of computer attacks on information security resources [of Russia](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Russia) ([State system of detection, prevention and elimination of consequences of computer attacks)](https://tadviser.com/index.php/State_system_of_detection,_prevention_and_elimination_of_consequences_of_computer_attacks), which will ensure the collection and exchange of information about [computer attacks.](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Cyber_attacks)

Simultaneously with the approval of the law "On the Security of Critical Information Infrastructure of the Russian Federation," amendments to the laws "On Communications," "On State Secrets," "On the Protection of the Rights of Legal Entities and Individual Entrepreneurs in the Implementation of State Control (Supervision) and Municipal Control," as well as amendments to the Criminal Code of the Russian Federation. Thus, in chapter 28 of the Criminal Code "Crimes in the field of computer information" article 274.1 will appear, which provides for punishment for harm caused to objects of critical information infrastructure.

### The State Duma allowed hackers to be imprisoned for 10 years for attacks on KII

In July 2017, the [Lower House of the Russian Parliament](https://tadviser.com/index.php/State_Duma_of_the_Federal_Assembly_of_the_Russian_Federation) adopted in the third, final, reading a bill criminalizing [computer attacks](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Cyber_attacks) on critical information infrastructure.[\[71\]](https://tadviser.com#cite_note-70)

The bill provides that the Criminal Code will include an article "Unlawful impact on the critical information infrastructure of the Russian Federation," the maximum punishment for which will be 10 years in prison with a ban on holding certain positions or engaging in certain activities.

[The Federation Council](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Federation_Council_of_the_Federal_Assembly_of_the_Russian_Federation) should approve the document and to sign to the Russian President. It is expected to take effect on January 1, 2018.

Criminal punishment for cyber attacks is part of a package of bills on critical information infrastructure submitted by the government to the State Duma in December 2016. The package of bills approved by the lower house of the Russian parliament, in particular, introduces the concept of "[critical information infrastructure](https://tadviser.com/index.php/CUES)" and establishes the powers [of government agencies](https://tadviser.com/index.php/State) to ensure its security.

## 2013: Decree of President Putin on the creation of a system for protecting information resources

In January 2013, President  [Vladimir Putin](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Vladimir_Putin) signed [a decree](http://base.consultant.ru/cons/cgi/online.cgi?req=doc;base=LAW;n=140909/) on the creation in [Russia of a system](https://tadviser.com/index.php/Russia) for detecting, preventing and eliminating the consequences of computer attacks on information resources located in the country and in diplomatic missions and consular offices of Russia abroad.

Its key tasks, in accordance with the presidential decree, should be to predict situations in the field of \[\[[information security](https://tadviser.com/index.php?title=Information_Security&action=edit&redlink=1), ensure the interaction of IT resource owners in solving problems related to the detection and elimination of computer attacks with telecom operators and other organizations engaged in information protection activities. The list of tasks of the system also includes assessing the degree of protection of critical [IT infrastructure](https://tadviser.com/index.php/IT_infrastructure) from computer attacks and establishing the causes of such incidents.

Putin instructed  [the FSB](https://tadviser.com/index.php/FSB) to organize work on the creation of a [state](https://tadviser.com/index.php/State) anti-hacker system [.](https://tadviser.com/index.php/FSB)

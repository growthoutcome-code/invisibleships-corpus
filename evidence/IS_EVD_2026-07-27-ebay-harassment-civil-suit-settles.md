## Captured text

Almost seven years after a group of eBay employees [harassed and stalked Ina and David Steiner](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2021/07/31/business/it-has-be-known-what-was-done-us-natick-couple-harassed-by-ebay-tell-their-story-first-time/), the Natick couple on Monday agreed to a final $55.7 million settlement of their [lawsuit against the company and some of its former top executives](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2021/07/21/business/natick-couple-stalked-harassed-by-ebay-files-suit/).

The couple’s law firm, Scalli Murphy Law, P.C., announced the settlement on Monday evening and filed a notice in US District Court in Boston dismissing all charges from the Steiners’ lawsuit.

The deal could bring some solace to the Steiners, whose quiet suburban life was ripped apart in 2019 when people working for eBay [bombarded them with online threats and bizarre deliveries](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2020/06/15/metro/six-former-ebay-employees-charged-federal-cyberstalking-case-targeting-natick-couple/) including a bloody pig mask, live spiders, and a funeral wreath. The scheme, which also included [following the couple around town and trying to attach a tracker to their car](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2022/03/17/business/bizarre-harassment-natick-couple-by-ebay-was-clearly-criminal-company-admitted/), started after eBay’s former CEO, Devin Wenig, complained about coverage of the company on the Steiners’ website, [EcommerceBytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/).

Seven people who worked for eBay [ultimately pleaded guilty to criminal charges](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2024/07/18/business/final-ebay-employee-avoids-prison-harassing-natick-couple/) (four served time in prison), and the company [paid a $3 million penalty](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2024/01/11/business/ebay-settles-criminal-charges-over-harassing-natick-couple-new-details-emerge/), but no senior executives were charged.

Now the e-commerce giant will pay the Steiners $46.15 million; former chief executive Wenig will pay the couple $2 million; former chief communications officer Steve Wymer will pay $50,000; and former chief of global operations Wendy Jones will pay $500,000.

The company also agreed to donate $6 million to various charities, and Wenig will donate $1 million to a charity dedicated to protecting First Amendment rights in the name of Ina Steiner.

“From the beginning, our clients wanted to uncover the truth, protect journalists and publishers, hold those responsible accountable, deter future corporate misconduct, and help ensure others would never have to endure what they experienced,” attorney Christopher Murphy said in a statement.

The company posted a [statement](https://www.ebayinc.com/stories/news/ebay-statement-regarding-agreement-with-ina-and-david-steiner/) admitting that the harassment was “wrong, reprehensible and should never have happened.”

“We condemn, in the strongest terms possible, the employees who perpetrated and pled guilty to criminal charges for the misconduct against Ina and David Steiner,” the company statement added. “We also acknowledge the unprofessional tone in internal communications demonstrated, to different degrees and number, by Mr. Wenig, Mr. Wymer, and Ms. Jones.”

Wenig continued to maintain that he was unaware of the harassment campaign.

“No one should ever have been subjected to what the Steiners endured in 2019, and I’m saddened by it, especially because it occurred during my time as CEO of eBay,” he said in a statement to the Globe. “The harassment was deliberately done in secret and without my knowledge, as established by an independent law firm review, a federal probe, and the sworn testimony of the security employee convicted as the ‘ringleader.’ This behavior is antithetical to everything I stand for and believe in.”

Lawyers for the other two defendants did not respond to requests for comment.

Unlike many settlements, the agreement does not include a confidentiality clause prohibiting the Steiners from speaking publicly. And eBay will issue “a strongly-worded public statement” regarding the conduct of its former executives, Murphy said.

The parties [announced an agreement in principle](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/02/25/business/ebay-settlement-harass-steiner/) in February, but tense negotiations had dragged on for months. In June, a lawyer for the Steiners told US Judge Patti Saris, overseeing the case, that a final settlement could not be reached, and Saris rescheduled a jury trial for early next year.

The stalking began as early as 2018, when Wenig complained to his communications team about critical coverage on the Steiners’ site, according to emails made public as part of a settlement the company struck with prosecutors to avoid charges. Jones, then senior vice president of global operations, wrote that the company should “clap back at Ina,” in one message. Wymer, hired at the beginning of 2019, told subordinates to “eff with her” and wrote “I want to blow her site up.”

Wenig’s complaints continued. In May 2019, he wrote to Wymer: “I couldn’t care less what she says” and “take her down,” in reference to Ina Steiner. And in an August 2019 text conversation, the CEO wrote: “If we are ever going to take her down ..now is the time.”

The articles that drew the eBay executives’ ire included reports on the CEO’s salary and his comments about protecting e-sellers from fraud. Prosecutors revealed angry text messages about an April 10, 2019, article titled “eBay CEO Devin Wenig Earns 152 Times That of Employees,” as well as “eBay CEO Says Sellers Can Expect Greater Protections,” from May 31, 2019. Such articles generated reader comments that were highly critical of the company’s leadership and its treatment of smaller sellers, who felt left behind in favor of larger retailers.

Wenig’s lawyers have argued during the lawsuit that the “take her down” comments have been taken out of context and referred to a planned publicity campaign strategy.

Wymer, in turn, relayed the complaints to Jim Baugh, a former CIA employee who eBay had tapped to head its global security unit. Baugh hatched the harassment campaign from his unit, including sending the Steiners threatening messages from fake Twitter accounts, live spiders and roaches, a bloody pig mask like the one in the horror movie “Saw,” and a funeral wreath.

On Aug. 15, 2019, Baugh and several of his employees traveled to Boston. They tried to install a GPS tracking device on the Steiners’ car and followed David Steiner around Natick.

After the Steiners reported to local police the license plate number of the rental car the eBay team was using to stalk them, the scheme unraveled.

Prosecutors charged Baugh and six of his subordinates with charges related to the stalking and harassment, and all pleaded guilty. Baugh was sentenced to 57 months in prison.

The company fired Wymer in 2019 after an internal investigation. Wenig resigned the same year, keeping $17 million of annual compensation and $40 million of severance. Jones retired from the company in 2020, making $16 million that year, according to court filings.

The Steiners filed their lawsuit in 2021.

The 2019 scheme “to intimidate, threaten, torture, terrorize, stalk, and silence the Steiners was successful and harmful,” the Steiners’ lawsuit stated. “The unrelenting stream of threats to kill, disturbing deliveries, as well as the physical surveillance caused the Steiners to suffer from significant and continuing emotional distress.”

The Steiners’ lawsuit alleged that Wenig had set the incidents in motion by sending texts to a subordinate criticizing Ecommercebytes and saying of Ina Steiner, “take her down.”

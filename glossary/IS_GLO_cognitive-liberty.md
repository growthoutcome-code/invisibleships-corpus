## cognitive liberty

Cognitive liberty is an evolving concept that asserts the fundamental right of an individual to have sovereignty over their own mind and consciousness. It goes beyond the traditional right to "freedom of thought" by addressing the new challenges posed by advances in neuroscience and technology.

The rise of technologies that directly interact with the human brain is making cognitive liberty a critical issue. These technologies include:

  - Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs): Devices that can read brain signals (e.g., to control a prosthetic limb) or send signals to the brain (e.g., to treat depression).
  - Neuro-enhancements: Technologies and drugs designed to improve memory, concentration, or other cognitive functions.
  - Artificial Intelligence (AI): AI algorithms and generative AI can be used to exploit cognitive biases, micro-target individuals with persuasive content, and even reconstruct thoughts from brain data, raising concerns about mental manipulation and privacy.

These developments pose a number of ethical and legal dilemmas. For example, if a company can monitor an employee's brain activity to measure productivity, is that a violation of cognitive liberty? Should the government be able to mandate "cognitive enhancement" for certain jobs?

Ultimately, cognitive liberty seeks to protect the individual's right to control their most private and intimate sphere—their mind. It is seen as a necessary foundation for all other human rights, as the ability to think freely is a prerequisite for freedom of speech, belief, and action.

# Concepts

*Recap written with AI assistance from the files listed below. It describes what the record contains; it is not part of the record and does not verify it. Read the source files before quoting.*

**Covers:** 51 concept files, 3 overview files and 3 data files in `concepts/` · about 50,000 words.

## What a concept is

A concept is a short essay that sets out one argument or question raised by the archive, with the sources it relies on. Each concept is one self-contained file (`IS_CON_<slug>.md`), and every header states four things:

- **BASIS**: how much weight the concept can bear.
- **ORIGIN**: `ai` (33 concepts, written with AI analysis of the datasets and public records) or `author` (18 concepts).
- **THEME**: which of seven topic groups it belongs to.
- **audience**: the readers it was written for (households, investigators, policy, clinicians, press).

Headers also carry `verification` (17 `verified`, 34 `unverified`; the overview lists the unverified ones as "Not independently verified") and `series`, which groups concepts into 10 reading-order series defined in `series.json`.

## The BASIS tiers

The tiers are ranked, and the files say they are never mixed within one concept. A reader can accept only the top tier and set the rest aside (IS-CON-00-START-HERE).

| Basis | Meaning, as the files state it | Count |
|---|---|---|
| `documented` | A source, ruling or official record supports it directly | 32 |
| `structural` | Follows from what a dataset does or does not contain | 11 |
| `pattern` | An observation drawn from experience, offered as an observation | 6 |
| `testimony` | A dated first-person report, verified by nobody | 2 |

Being `documented` does not make a concept `verified`. Twenty-three documented concepts are marked "Not independently verified", and seven of those have author origin.

## The headline findings and the limits

`IS_CON_00_findings.md` (IS-CON-00-FINDINGS) collects eight headline figures. Each one points to the concept that sources it. They cover:

- regulator fines against one facial-recognition company abroad, set beside a US federal contract with the same company
- a health-data breach at a clearing house
- a list of schoolchildren kept by a local sheriff's office, and that office's written settlement
- newspaper closures and the counties left with no local news
- a lawful trade in donated body parts
- the US suicide rate set against the world rate
- a laboratory study in which a delayed robot movement produced the feeling that someone was present

The file says none of the figures is a finding about any individual, including the author.

`IS_CON_00_not-established.md` (IS-CON-00-NOT-ESTABLISHED) sets out four limits that apply to every concept:

- nothing establishes that anything specific was done to a specific person
- no capability is documented that reads perception or reaches a person at a distance without their participation
- an absence of evidence is recorded only as an absence
- sources placed side by side do not corroborate one another

The file calls itself "the file to read first if you are here to check this archive" (IS-CON-00-FINDINGS).

## How the material divides

- **Documented, AI-origin concepts** cover public records: neural-data law and who owns neural data, what brain-computer interfaces can and cannot decode, non-surgical interface research, organised-harassment cases decided by courts, procurement and fines, medical-record protection, children and surveillance, and nanotechnology (for example IS-CON-CAN-A-MACHINE-READ-THOUGHT, IS-CON-ORGANISED-HARASSMENT-IS-FACT).
- **Structural concepts** are about the datasets themselves: there is no column for the individual, accountability is not linked to deployment, headline spending is not actual spending, and sequence cannot be proven (IS-CON-NO-COLUMN-FOR-YOU, IS-CON-CO-OCCURRENCE-IS-NOT-CAUSE).
- **Author-origin pattern and testimony concepts** set out the author's hypotheses and account, such as "Diving", "Do Not Choose Yourself", the "neurotech bullhorn" and "Has an attack happened?". Their headers say they are observation or first-person report.

`source-years.csv` lists 34 dated sources cited by concepts, with the concept files that cite each one.

## Before asking questions of it

Check a concept's `basis`, `origin` and `verification` before relying on it. Concepts cite named organisations because their sources do. The terms file says that a citation is not an accusation, and that two sources sitting together do not support each other.

They are not official journal entries and should not be quoted or cited as such (see the folder README and the `status` and `official: false` fields in each header). ## How it is organised
There is one file per note, named by date and time (IDs `IS-JDN-<date>-<time>`). There are 11 notes from April and 11 from May. Several days have two or three notes (9, 24 and 28 April; 1, 3 and 23 May). Most notes were written at or after nights at a downtown men's shelter, and several are headed by their location. Lengths range from about 60 words (IS-JDN-20260409-0838) to about 1,900 (IS-JDN-20260504-1030). Many end with the author's Questions & Comments, as journal entries do.

## Main recurring themes
- **Requests to accept euthanization.** Notes record continued statements asking whether the author "needs" euthanization, with the wording noted as changing (IS-JDN-20260410-0924, IS-JDN-20260416-1014, IS-JDN-20260420-1408).
- **Point-of-view scenes of violence.** The author describes many scenes he experienced from another person's viewpoint, including executions and killings, and repeatedly asks whether they are real, staged or "manufactured evidence" against him (IS-JDN-20260501-1016, IS-JDN-20260504-1030, IS-JDN-20260428-1219).
- **Being asked to locate people.** Speakers show him photographs and ask him to find the people in them; he later says he will not do this without in-person law-enforcement sponsorship (IS-JDN-20260409-0930, IS-JDN-20260424-1251).
- **Statements about his family.** Several notes record statements and scenes saying his former wife and daughter have been harmed. Each time, the author sets against them the only information he treats as reliable: an attorney's statement that the family is "alive and well" (IS-JDN-20260424-1251, IS-JDN-20260503-1109).
- **Sexual content and hate speech.** Notes record unwanted sexual sensations and imagery at night, and anti-gay statements aimed at the author and a shelter neighbour; the author states his own responses (IS-JDN-20260410-0924, IS-JDN-20260503-0926, IS-JDN-20260517-0934).
- **Language and translation.** From May the author asks connected people "What language are you speaking?", and records answers that he takes as evidence of automatic translation (IS-JDN-20260501-1016, IS-JDN-20260503-0926).
- **Announcements and claimed authority.** Notes record a "public announcement" telling Denver that what people experience is technology, and statements about an announcement by a federal agency, alongside threats (IS-JDN-20260420-1408, IS-JDN-20260501-1412).
- **Places and institutions named by speakers.** Statements refer to a mountain county, a detention center, foreign countries, a remote continent, and technology and pharmaceutical companies. The notes record that these were said; they make no finding about any of them (IS-JDN-20260428-1219, IS-JDN-20260509-0625, IS-JDN-20260517-0934).

## How it changes over time
The April notes are mostly overnight statement lists and short reports. From 1 May the notes grow longer and centre on extended point-of-view scenes, many of them violent, together with the author's translation questions. The last notes are short. One records statements about an identity-verification service, and a companion note pastes in an AI research answer about where that service keeps data (IS-JDN-20260523-0919, IS-JDN-20260523-0928). The final note describes a scene from the night and statements heard on waking (IS-JDN-20260524-0625). The author's own position stays the same throughout: the statements need verification, he asks for contact by email or phone and for news-media acknowledgement, and he is isolating to protect friends, family and his former employer (IS-JDN-20260428-1258).

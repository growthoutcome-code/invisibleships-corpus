# The Invisible Ships archive — overview

*Recap written with AI assistance from the archive's own files and the recaps in this folder. It describes what the record contains; it is not part of the record and does not verify it. Read the source files before quoting.*

## What this archive is
This is the research archive behind invisibleships.com, compiled by Sean C. Harris ("the author").

Its core is a four-part journal, kept from late 2024 to May 2026. The journal records statements the author heard and transcribed. The archive presents them as **external communications from an LRAD, RF or EMF source, without proof**. Around the journal sit:
- reference documents, glossaries and concepts (the archive's arguments, each labelled by how much weight it can bear)

## The journal at a glance
**How it was recorded**
- **Until June 2025:** typed, timestamped logs, often paraphrased.
- **From July 2025:** the author repeats the statements aloud into a phone recorder and transcribes them with an AI tool. Most recordings are from shelters in Denver and from a street intersection where he holds a sign.
- **January–February 2026:** typed entries return.
- **April–May 2026:** a final set of recordings and notes.

**Recurring statements, across all four Parts**

- Statements telling drivers not to support him, and saying that those who respond are added to a "euthanization list".

- Reactions to being recorded.
- In later Parts, more supportive and defensive voices, and "override" statements.

**The author's own position**, stated throughout: the statements are unproven, he accuses no organisation, and he asks for verification, testimony and official acknowledgment.

- **The journal:** it is a dated record of what was heard and transcribed. It does not prove who, if anyone, produced the statements.
- **The research datasets:** these hold sourced public figures, each tiered A (documented), B (corroborated) or C (claimed). They do not corroborate each other or the journal.

## Author's plans and AI analysis

The documents mix two kinds of writing, and the files show which is which:

- **Written or directed by the author.** The opening sections of `plan-for-justice/`, `neurotech-law-enforcement/` and `llm-analysis/` share the same title page and introduction in the author's voice (IS-REF-PLAN-FOR-JUSTICE-01). `personal-protection-plan/` opens with the author's own overview and a last-updated date (its section 01).
- **AI-generated responses.** `plan-for-justice/` section 04 is the author's prompt to a large language model, and section 05 is headed "Prompt Response". Most of what follows reads as the model's replies: twenty sections are headed only "Yes" or "Yes, absolutely", which are the opening words of a reply rather than section titles. `llm-analysis/` section 06 states that it was added by an AI model on a dated day. Three `personal-protection-plan/` sections are headed "DTT Agent Report" (sections 17–19). The research report in `neurotech-law-enforcement/` section 04 and the two comparison reports (`asset-protection-comparison/`, `impact-zersetzung/`) are written in the third person "for the author" and read as commissioned analysis.

AI-generated sections are analysis and drafting. They are not findings, and they carry the same disclaimer as everything else in the archive.

## Recurring themes

- Building a legal record: affidavits, notices of liability, discovery lists, and lists of laws the documents say could apply (`plan-for-justice/` sections 11–16, 40, 46).
- Protecting against being misdiagnosed: the author's prompt asks for documentation that protects a person who reports this experience from a mental-health diagnosis or involuntary commitment, and several sections draft cross-examination outlines for that setting (sections 04, 06, 17, 18).
- Household and family safety: verification protocols, guidance for minors, and "in-person drills" (sections 11, 23, 39, 54).
- Recording and measurement: hardware lists, signal checklists, and the cost of forensic surveys (`personal-protection-plan/` sections 14, 16, 17; `plan-for-justice/` sections 78–82).
- Grouping the external statements by theme, including statements referencing euthanization (`llm-analysis/` sections 06–13).
